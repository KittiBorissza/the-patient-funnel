Kitti Borissza · the plan behind my application

The plan behind it

In every market Invity serves, plenty of people are curious about Bitcoin, yet the first purchase can feel like an exam they haven’t studied for. I want Invity’s social channels to make that step understandable in each market’s own language and context, so people can decide calmly, yes or no.

Independent application proposal. Not an official Invity page. Every number below is a synthetic example, not Invity data.

How it works. Cost per install rises as a channel spends more: base × (1 + 0.15 × spend ÷ €1,000) ÷ scenario factor. First buyers = installs × buy rate, where the scenario factor (Cautious 0.7, Base 1, Upside 1.3) also scales the buy rate, capped at 100%. Referrals are capped per market, because only so many existing customers will invite someone; referral money beyond that is reported as unused. Each market multiplies the base cost per install by its own synthetic cost index. Local rules and ad approvals differ, so each market’s channels need checking before any money moves. Media and rewards only: no salaries or production. A low spend per first buyer doesn’t prove profit or that the buyers wouldn’t have come anyway.

What I’d test

Seven creatives, each with one question to answer

Each has a hypothesis and one metric, and each runs in every market with the same test so results can be compared. Copy is localised per market: a native speaker checks every local version, Invity’s compliance checks claims, and ad approvals are checked per platform and market. Hungarian is the one language I can check myself.

First 30 days

The first month

  • Weeks 1–2: talk to support, product and the people who know each market; read the comments in every language we can.
  • Set up the same measurement in every market at once, with whoever owns analytics, so results can be compared.
  • Pick two creatives and run the same tests in every market in parallel, localised and checked by native speakers and compliance.
  • Run a significance check per market, per test, and treat small samples as too close to call.
  • End of month: share what worked where, what didn’t, and what I’d repeat before acting on it.
