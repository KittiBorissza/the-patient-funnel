# MiCA wording note

_Written for v2.0.0, 9 Nov 2026. Not legal advice._

**The v1.1.0 page said:** "From 1 July 2026, only MiCA-licensed firms can serve EU crypto clients." This sentence is taken from Invity's own blog post of 24 June 2026 ("MiCA and the July 1, 2026 deadline", https://www.invity.io/blog/mica-july-2026-deadline-what-it-means-for-your-bitcoin).

**What authoritative sources say**
- MiCA Article 143(3): providers that served clients under national law before 30 December 2024 "may continue to do so until 1 July 2026 or until they are granted or refused an authorisation pursuant to Article 63, whichever is sooner". Member States could shorten this. (ESMA interactive single rulebook, https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-143-transitional-measures)
- ESMA, Statement on the end of transitional periods under MiCA (April 2026): the transitional period expires across the EU on 1 July 2026; after that, entities providing crypto-asset services to EU clients without MiCA authorisation must stop. It also notes the narrow reverse-solicitation exception for non-EU firms. (https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-04/ESMA75-113276571-1679%5FStatement%5Fon%5Fthe%5Fend%5Fof%5Ftransitional%5Fperiods%5Funder%5FMiCA.pdf)
- ESMA public statement (June 2026) on orderly wind-down of unauthorised CASPs. (https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/ESMA75-113276571-1710_Public_Statement_MiCA_transitional_period_ends.pdf)

**Nuance the short sentence skips**
- Some regulated financial entities (for example credit institutions and investment firms) can provide certain crypto-asset services after notifying their regulator under MiCA Article 60, without a separate CASP authorisation. "Only MiCA-licensed firms" is therefore a simplification.
- Reverse solicitation (Article 61) is a narrow exception.

**Safer wording if needed:** "The EU's MiCA transition period ended on 1 July 2026. Since then, firms offering crypto services in the EU need authorisation under MiCA (or to fall under one of its narrow exceptions)."

**Decision for v2.0.0:** left off the main page. It isn't needed to make the case, and how Invity talks about regulation is for Invity's compliance team to decide.
