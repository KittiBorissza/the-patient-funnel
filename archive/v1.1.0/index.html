Smallest viable audience: the Czech pilot

People in Czechia who follow Czech Bitcoin creators, podcasts or meetups, or who opened an Invity account and never bought. Small enough to talk to by name in the comments. Big enough to read a result in a month.

  • Home market: Prague team, Czech interface, licensed by the Czech National Bank under MiCA. invity.io, 9 Nov 2026
  • An active local Bitcoin scene and the SatoshiLabs and Trezor heritage people already recognise. invity.io “SatoshiLabs family”
  • Shortest loop from a post, to a question in the comments, to an answer from the product team. My inference

Germany second, once the Czech loop works: larger audience, German interface, higher media costs. My inference

What would change my mind: internal data showing a higher first-purchase rate at similar cost in another market, or fewer than five credible Czech Bitcoin creators willing to show a real setup.

Auto Buy first. Turbo Buy on its own track.

Auto Buy is the simplest promise and the base the other strategies build on. Every acquisition test in the first 30 days points to it.

Turbo Buy adds 60% extra buying power to each recurring buy, secured by the bitcoin in the plan. invity.io/turbo-buy That needs its own track:

  • aimed at existing Auto Buy users, not cold audiences;
  • risk explained in the same post as the benefit;
  • its own compliance review and its own metrics;
  • no Turbo Buy in paid acquisition until compliance agrees how it may be advertised. Needs internal data
What would change my mind: cohorts that start with a one-off buy staying active longer than cohorts that start with Auto Buy.

One remarkable thing per two-week sprint

Something worth talking about, made once and well, instead of more posts.

  • Sprint 1 · The whole first buy. One unedited screen recording of a Prague team member setting up a small Auto Buy: amount, schedule, the fee screen, the custody choice. Nobody shows the boring part.
  • Sprint 2 · The question wall. The 30 questions people actually asked in comments and DMs, each answered in one line, then turned into the FAQ.
  • Sprint 3 · Withdraw on camera. Moving bitcoin from the app to a hardware wallet, step by step. Self-custody shown, not just promised.
  • Sprint 4 · What can go wrong. A frank page on volatility and on how Turbo Buy settles if the price falls. Reviewed by compliance before it ships.

Sprint ideas depend on product and compliance sign-off. Needs internal data

Permission, not interruption

In CRM and community, people choose what they hear from us.

  • At sign-up and in the newsletter, ask once: product changes only, a monthly letter, a six-part beginner series, or account messages only.
  • Every message carries a visible “Send me less” link next to unsubscribe. Less is a choice, not a loss.
  • Frequency cap per person, agreed with the CRM owner.
  • Unsubscribes, spam complaints and “send less” clicks are guardrail metrics in every test.
  • In community: answer in public, move account issues to support, never DM first.

02c · Education as the growth engine

Teach first. Then invite the first buy.

Invity already ships education. Social’s job is to turn those assets into hooks, consent-based leads, and activation — not to invent a second curriculum.

What already exists Public evidence

  • Invity Academy — in-app bite-sized lessons, quizzes and frameworks. About page: “Get smarter about Bitcoin.” Timeline on About us places an educational Academy in the 2024 product era; a Czech Academy launch post is dated 18 Feb 2025 and describes short self-paced lessons spanning history of money, Bitcoin and blockchain, plus financial literacy (exact in-app lesson titles not published on the English site — do not invent names). invity.io/about-us · blog.invity.io Academy post, 18 Feb 2025
  • Blog — product, strategy and education on invity.io/blog (and the older blog.invity.io host). Directory hub: How to Bitcoin (9 Jan 2025).
  • Newsletter · “Straight from Invity” — regular Bitcoin, finance and Invity updates with explicit subscribe consent on About us. invity.io/about-us
  • eBook · “Broken Money and the Way Out” — free ~50-page beginner guide; Invity Academy Volume 01; landing /broken-money-and-the-way-out; PDF download.

How social uses them

  1. Academy → content hooks. One public question per week that a quiz or short lesson already answers; CTA is “open Academy in the app,” not a price tip.
  2. Quiz-to-lead. After an educational Meta/X piece, offer the eBook or newsletter only with clear marketing consent (GDPR). Soft gate: email → PDF / newsletter, never a forced purchase.
  3. eBook as lead magnet. Paid and organic point to the Broken Money landing; UTM’d; measure confirmed subscribers and later first purchases, not vanity downloads alone.
  4. Blog → carousel / thread. One verified article becomes 5–7 slides or a thread (custody, seed phrases, phishing, first buy). Link the article; keep claims inside what the post says.
  5. Academy completion as activation metric. Alongside first purchase: share of new sign-ups who finish at least one Academy path in 14 days. Needs internal data
  6. Newsletter as nurture. “Straight from Invity” carries the monthly Ask Invity Live recap and One Question answers — permission-first frequency.
What would change my mind: Academy completion correlating poorly with first purchase after we have 90 days of cohorts — then education stays brand, not activation.

05 · Content system

The calm Bitcoiner from Prague.

Knows Bitcoin well. Explains it without hype. Happy to say what can go wrong. The goal is enchantment through usefulness: people should leave a post knowing one thing they didn’t.

Seven rules

  1. One real question per post.
  2. Numbers beat adjectives. “€50 every Monday”, not “effortless wealth”.
  3. Risk in the same breath. Every product post carries the risk line.
  4. No price talk. No predictions, no charts as bait.
  5. Bitcoin only, and literate: stack, sats, self-custody, DCA, each explained once.
  6. Local faces. Czech first, English second.
  7. Dry humour about Bitcoin culture, never about losses or anyone’s money.

Standard risk line: “Bitcoin can lose value. Regular buying does not guarantee a profit.”

Meta Advertising Standards

Prior written permission required for ads promoting crypto exchanges/trading, lending, or wallets with buy/sell/swap/staking, and for customer-solicitation “investment” style ads (incl. some affiliates). Submit a licence Meta accepts via Business Suite. Open risk: Meta’s published accepted-licence list (read 9 Nov 2026) does not explicitly name Czech CNB / MiCA CASP — confirm with Meta whether Invity’s CNB authorisation is accepted before CZ/EU spend.

X Ads vs X Paid Partnerships

X Ads: crypto exchanges/wallets etc. allowed with country-specific licence + X certification; ICOs/mining banned; some countries unavailable (secondary summaries). X Paid Partnerships (creator): secondary sources (Mar 2026) report financial products including crypto ineligible in EU, UK, AU. X Help Center returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch — treat as verify-on-login before any EU KOL spend on X. Organic brand posts ≠ paid partnerships.

EU MiCA marketing

Art. 66 (CASPs): fair, clear, not misleading information including marketing (identified as such); warn of risks; publish pricing policies. Art. 7: mainly offer/admission marketing for crypto-assets other than ART/EMT (white-paper consistency + prescribed disclaimer) — still a useful honesty bar for social. Art. 6: white-paper content (not a social checklist). July 1, 2026 transitional messaging: use Invity’s own explainer; no scare tactics.

Hungary (MNB), DE/CZ notes

MNB materials restate MiCA marketing fairness and white-paper-linked rules for offers. Invity’s researched passport list does not include HU — don’t run HU acquisition as if passported. DE: BaFin appears on Meta/X licence examples; lead with accurate licence + Trezor lineage, not invented bank partnerships. CZ: CNB home licence; Bank iD onboarding is a public product story (Jul 2026 blog).

DSA · GDPR · ePrivacy · UCPD

This microsite: cookie-free, no pixels, no third-party trackers, no consent banner required for analytics we don’t load. Production Invity ads: Meta/X pixels need ePrivacy + GDPR consent before set; DSA Art. 26 ad transparency is the platform’s duty for paid ads. UCPD / influencers: clear paid-partnership disclosure; no false urgency or guaranteed gains.
